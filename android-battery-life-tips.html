How to Improve Android Battery Life: 12 Practical Tips

By Khan Tech Review · 7 min read · Updated November 9, 2026

Does your Android phone run out of charge before the day ends? You may not need a new phone. Display settings, weak mobile signal, background activity, demanding apps and battery age can all affect how long a charge lasts.

This guide explains practical settings to check, how to identify apps using unusual amounts of power, and how to look after your phone's battery. Menu names vary by manufacturer and Android version, so use the Settings search bar if a step looks different on your device.

1. Adjust screen brightness and screen timeout

The display can be one of the biggest power users, especially when brightness is high or the screen stays on when you are not using it.

  • Lower brightness indoors and in darker rooms.
  • Turn on Adaptive Brightness if it behaves well on your phone.
  • Set Screen timeout to a shorter interval, such as 30 seconds, if convenient.
  • If your phone has an always-on display, try turning it off or limiting its schedule.

Look under Settings → Display. Exact labels differ between devices.

2. Find apps that use the most battery

Instead of closing every app, first identify which ones are actually consuming unusual amounts of power.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Battery, then Battery usage or a similarly named option.
  3. Review the apps near the top of the list.
  4. Check whether their battery use matches how much you use them.

If an app you rarely open is using a lot of power, update it, review its permissions and background settings, or contact the app developer if the problem continues. High usage can be normal for games, video calls, navigation and camera apps.

3. Review refresh rate and display features

Some phones support 90Hz, 120Hz or higher refresh rates. These make scrolling feel smoother, but can use more energy than a standard refresh rate in some situations.

  • Try Adaptive or Auto refresh rate if available.
  • If battery life matters more than very smooth scrolling, test the Standard setting.
  • On OLED phones, a dark theme may reduce display power in some apps and situations.

Compare your battery usage over a normal day before deciding which setting works best for you.

4. Use Battery Saver or Power Saving Mode

Battery Saver can extend remaining charge by limiting background activity, some visual effects, syncing or performance features. The exact changes depend on your phone.

  • Turn it on when you need your battery to last longer.
  • Check whether your phone can enable it automatically at a chosen battery level.
  • Remember that some notifications or background updates may be delayed while it is active.

Usually found under Settings → Battery → Battery Saver or Power Saving Mode.

5. Manage mobile signal and wireless connections

A phone may use extra power when it struggles to maintain a mobile connection, such as in a basement or an area with weak coverage.

  • Use Wi-Fi when it is reliable and available.
  • Turn off mobile hotspot, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi when you are not using them, if they are actively consuming power.
  • If 5G coverage is weak where you spend time, test an available automatic or LTE network option and compare results.
  • Use Airplane mode in places where you do not need calls or mobile data and signal is unavailable.

6. Limit unnecessary background activity

Some apps need background access for messages, email, backups or health and safety features. Others may not need to refresh constantly.

  • Review background battery settings for apps you rarely use.
  • Restrict background use only when you are comfortable with delayed updates or notifications.
  • Uninstall apps you no longer need.

Avoid repeatedly force-stopping every app. Android manages memory and background processes itself, and force-stopping apps can interrupt notifications or normal functions.

7. Review location access

Navigation, ride-hailing, weather and fitness apps may use location services. Check which apps have access and whether they genuinely need it all the time.

  • Open Settings → Location → App location permissions, or use Settings search.
  • Choose “Allow only while using the app” where that meets your needs.
  • Keep location access enabled for essential safety, navigation or device-finding features.

8. Keep Android and apps updated

Updates can include bug fixes, security improvements and performance changes. An app or system bug may sometimes cause unusual battery drain.

  • Check Settings → System → Software update, or your manufacturer's equivalent.
  • Update apps through the official app store.
  • After a major update, give the phone some time to finish setup and background optimization before judging battery life.

9. Reduce widgets and live features you do not use

Some home-screen widgets, live wallpapers and frequently refreshing features may use background resources. Remove only the ones you do not find useful, then compare battery life.

10. Avoid excessive heat

Heat can speed up long-term battery ageing. Phones can become warm during gaming, navigation, video recording or charging, but avoid adding unnecessary heat.

  • Keep the phone out of direct sunlight and hot vehicles.
  • Avoid demanding games while the phone is charging if it becomes very hot.
  • If the device repeatedly becomes unusually hot during light use, check battery usage and seek manufacturer support.

11. Use sensible charging habits

Use a compatible, reputable charger and cable. If your phone offers Adaptive Charging, Optimized Charging or a battery protection limit, consider enabling it according to your routine.

You do not need to fully drain a modern phone battery before charging it. Avoid heat and follow the charging guidance provided by your phone manufacturer.

12. Check battery condition and troubleshoot sudden drain

If battery life has become much worse, consider whether the battery is ageing or whether a recent app or update changed power use.

  • Restart the phone if battery drain suddenly changes.
  • Check Battery usage for an app that recently started consuming more power.
  • Install pending app and system updates.
  • Use your manufacturer's battery diagnostics or support service if available.

If the phone's battery is swollen, damaged or unusually hot, stop using or charging it and contact an authorized repair provider. Do not try to open or puncture the battery.

Brand-specific Android settings: Samsung, Xiaomi and Pixel

Android manufacturers organize their settings differently. These are useful places to start, but the names and available options can change with model, region and software version.

Samsung Galaxy

Start in Settings → Battery to review battery usage and power-saving options. On some Galaxy models, background usage limits and battery protection are available in the Battery settings area.

Xiaomi and Redmi

Look in Settings → Battery for battery usage and battery-saver controls. Some models also provide app-specific battery settings. Use Settings search if the menu differs.

Google Pixel

Open Settings → Battery to review battery usage and Battery Saver. Depending on the model and Android version, Adaptive Battery and charging optimization options may also be available.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Android battery draining so fast?

Common causes include high screen brightness, demanding apps, weak mobile signal, background activity, software bugs, heat and an ageing battery. Check Battery usage first to narrow down the cause.

Does dark mode save battery?

It can reduce display power on many OLED screens because dark pixels use less power. The effect is usually smaller on LCD screens and depends on brightness and app design.

Should I close all apps to save battery?

No. Constantly force-closing apps is not usually necessary. Focus on apps with unusually high battery use and restrict background activity only when appropriate.

Is it okay to charge my phone overnight?

Many modern phones include charging controls designed to manage overnight charging. Use a compatible charger, keep the phone in a cool, ventilated place and enable optimized charging if available.

When should I replace my Android battery?

Consider a professional battery check if the phone has significantly reduced runtime, unexpected shutdowns or a battery-health warning. A swollen or damaged battery needs prompt professional attention.

Final thoughts

Improving Android battery life is usually about finding the settings and apps that matter most on your own phone. Start with battery usage, screen brightness and timeout, then review background activity, signal conditions and Battery Saver. Change one or two settings at a time so you can tell what actually helps.

Battery results vary by phone model, battery age, network coverage, software and personal usage. No single setting guarantees a particular number of extra hours.
