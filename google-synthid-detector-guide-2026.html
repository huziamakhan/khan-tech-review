Google SynthID Detector 2026: How to Detect AI-Generated Images, Videos and Audio

AI-generated media is becoming harder to distinguish from real content. Google's SynthID technology offers another way to check supported images, videos and audio—but it is important to understand what a positive or negative result actually means.

By Khan Tech Review  |  Published November 23, 2026  |  8–10 min read
Quick answer: SynthID is Google's watermarking technology for identifying supported AI-generated or AI-edited content. A SynthID check can provide useful evidence, but it is not a universal AI detector and it cannot prove that every unmarked file is human-made.

1. What Is Google SynthID Detector?

Google SynthID Detector refers to Google's media-verification experience built around SynthID, a technology that embeds an imperceptible digital watermark into content created or edited with supported AI tools. The watermark is designed to remain detectable even when media is changed in common ways, such as being compressed, cropped, filtered or resized.

Instead of judging only what an image looks like, a SynthID check looks for a signal embedded in the media. Depending on the supported tool and file type, this can help identify whether an image, video or audio clip contains content generated or edited by participating AI systems.

Google announced wider availability of its SynthID Detector experience in October 2026, with English-language access and support described for Google AI and participating partners. Availability and supported formats can change, so check the official SynthID website for the latest access details.

2. How Does SynthID Watermarking Work?

When a supported AI system creates or edits media, it can embed a digital signal into the output. The watermark is designed to be difficult for people to notice during normal viewing or listening. A compatible detector then analyses the file for that signal.

For images, the signal is embedded in visual data. For audio, it is incorporated into the audio signal. For video, the system can examine the visual information across frames and look for the relevant watermark patterns. The exact implementation varies by media type.

Why is an invisible watermark useful?

  • It does not need a visible label: people can view or listen to the media normally.
  • It can survive some edits: the technology is designed to remain detectable after common transformations, although detection is not guaranteed in every case.
  • It adds provenance information: it can provide evidence about the AI system involved in creating or editing supported media.
  • It complements other checks: it can be used alongside source verification and digital content credentials.

3. How to Check AI-Generated Images, Videos and Audio

If the SynthID Detector service is available to you, start from Google's official SynthID website. Follow the current upload and verification instructions shown there. Do not upload private, confidential or sensitive media unless you have reviewed the service's privacy information and are comfortable sharing the file.

Step 1: Open the official detector

Type synthid.com into your browser or use the official link above. Check that the address is correct before uploading anything.

Step 2: Choose the supported media type

Select an image, video or audio file if that format is currently supported by the service. File-size, duration and format restrictions may apply.

Step 3: Upload the file you want to check

Use the original file when possible. Repeatedly downloaded, screen-recorded or heavily edited copies may contain less useful information for verification.

Step 4: Review the detector's explanation

Read the result carefully. Look for the section or time range where a watermark signal was detected, if the tool provides that information.

Step 5: Verify the source independently

Check who first published the media, whether a reliable source confirms the event, and whether the original context has been preserved. A watermark result is only one part of a responsible fact-check.

4. Can You Check AI Images and Videos with Gemini?

Google also provides SynthID-related verification inside Gemini for supported content. You can upload a supported file and ask a direct question, such as:

Example prompt: “Was this image, video or audio created or edited using Google AI? Please explain whether you found a SynthID watermark.”

Gemini's in-chat verification is not identical to every feature of the standalone SynthID Detector. Google's Gemini help information describes checking for SynthID in media generated or edited with Google AI, and it also discusses Content Credentials as a separate source of provenance information. The standalone detector's partner coverage may be broader, depending on the current rollout.

For the latest supported file limits and instructions, read the official Gemini Help guidance on identifying AI-generated media. Limits and supported features can change over time.

5. What Do SynthID Detector Results Mean?

The most important point is that a detector result should not be treated as a complete verdict about truth or authenticity.

Possible resultWhat it may meanWhat you should do
Watermark detectedThe file, or part of it, may have been generated or edited by a supported AI system that embeds a recognised SynthID watermark.Check the identified section and confirm the original source and context.
No watermark detectedThe detector did not find a supported SynthID signal in the submitted file.Do not assume the content is human-made. It may come from another AI tool or have no detectable watermark.
Inconclusive or unsupportedThe file type, quality, format or available signal may not allow a reliable check.Try an original supported file, or use other provenance and fact-checking methods.

Remember: AI-generated does not automatically mean fake. An AI-created illustration, music track or edited photograph can be legitimate. The key question is whether the content is being presented honestly and in the right context.

6. Important Limitations: Is SynthID 100% Accurate?

No detection system should be treated as perfect. SynthID is designed to find its own supported watermark signals; it is not a magic scanner that recognises every image, video or audio file produced by every AI company.

  • It is not universal: media made with tools that do not embed a compatible SynthID watermark may not be identified.
  • A negative result is not proof: “No watermark found” does not establish that a person created the file.
  • Editing can complicate checks: severe transformations, very short clips, low-quality files or other processing may affect detection.
  • Coverage varies: different Google products and partner integrations may support different media types and sources.
  • It does not judge truth: a watermark cannot tell you whether a caption, claim or story attached to a file is accurate.

Use SynthID as one useful signal, not as the only basis for accusing someone of creating a fake or for deciding whether a news story is true.

7. Other Ways to Spot Suspicious AI Media

When a file has no detectable watermark, use a broader verification process. These steps are helpful for social media posts, breaking-news clips, viral photos and suspicious voice messages.

  1. Find the earliest source: search for the original post, photographer, publisher or organisation that released the file.
  2. Use reverse image search: compare the image with older versions and look for the earliest known publication.
  3. Check the context: confirm the date, location, people and event with independent reliable sources.
  4. Listen carefully to audio: unusual pauses, unnatural pronunciation or inconsistent background sound can be warning signs, but they are not proof.
  5. Look for independent confirmation: for important claims, seek confirmation from reputable news organisations or official sources.
  6. Do not share first and verify later: avoid forwarding unverified media, especially when it could harm someone's reputation or safety.

8. SynthID vs. Traditional AI Image Detector Tools

Many online AI image detector tools estimate whether an image looks AI-generated by analysing visual patterns. SynthID follows a different approach when the relevant watermark is present: it checks for an embedded signal associated with supported generation or editing systems.

Neither method should be used alone to make a serious accusation. Whenever possible, combine technical checks with original-source research and trustworthy reporting.

9. Why AI Media Verification Matters in 2026

AI tools can now generate convincing pictures, voices, music and video clips in a short time. This creates useful opportunities for education, entertainment and creative work, but it also makes it easier to circulate misleading media, impersonate people or remove important context from a real recording.

Watermarking and content-provenance tools are part of a wider effort to make digital media easier to examine. They do not replace media literacy, journalism or platform moderation. Instead, they give people another piece of information when deciding whether to trust and share something.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is Google SynthID Detector?

It is Google's media verification experience for checking supported images, videos and audio for SynthID watermarks associated with participating AI systems.

Can SynthID detect every AI-generated image?

No. It checks for supported watermark signals. AI media created without a compatible watermark may not be detected.

Does a SynthID watermark mean a picture is fake?

No. It can indicate AI involvement, but AI-generated or AI-edited media is not automatically false or misleading.

Can I use Gemini to check an image?

Yes, Gemini supports SynthID-related checks for eligible media generated or edited with Google AI. Follow Google's current file and upload guidance.

Is SynthID free?

Access and usage conditions depend on the specific Google experience and its current availability. Check the official SynthID website and Gemini Help for current details.

What should I do if no watermark is found?

Treat the result as inconclusive about human authorship. Check the original source, search for earlier versions and look for independent confirmation.

Final Verdict

Google SynthID Detector is a useful development in digital media verification because it can help identify supported AI-generated or AI-edited content through embedded watermarks. It is especially valuable when people need an additional clue about where an image, video or audio clip came from.

However, it is not a universal AI image detector, and a missing watermark does not prove that content is real or human-made. For the best results, combine SynthID checks with source verification, reverse image search and reliable reporting. Think before sharing—and never treat a single detector result as the whole story.
