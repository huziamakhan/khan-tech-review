How to Protect Your WhatsApp Account from Hackers and Scams (2026)

By Khan Tech Review | 8–10 min read | Published November 18, 2026

WhatsApp is an important communication tool for millions of people. We use it to talk with friends, share photos, send documents and communicate with businesses. However, scammers and cybercriminals also target WhatsApp users through fake messages, phishing links, verification-code tricks and unauthorized device connections.

The good news is that you can reduce many of these risks by using the security features already available in WhatsApp and following a few simple habits. In this guide, we explain how to protect your WhatsApp account from hackers and scams in 2026.

1. Why WhatsApp Account Security Matters

Your WhatsApp account may contain personal conversations, contact information, photos, documents and other private communications. If someone gains unauthorized access, they could impersonate you, contact your friends or attempt to trick people into sending money.

Many account takeover attempts do not involve complicated hacking. Instead, criminals try to convince users to share a verification code, scan a suspicious QR code or link an unknown device.

Important: A message claiming that your WhatsApp account will be blocked unless you verify it through an unfamiliar link should be treated as suspicious.

2. Enable Two-Step Verification

Two-step verification adds an additional security layer when registering your phone number with WhatsApp. It helps prevent unauthorized people from taking over your account, even if they obtain your SMS verification code.

How to enable it

  1. Open WhatsApp on your phone.
  2. Go to Settings.
  3. Select Account or the security option shown in your version.
  4. Open Two-step verification.
  5. Follow the instructions to set up the additional password or PIN.
  6. Add a recovery email if the option is available.

WhatsApp announced an upgrade from the traditional six-digit two-step verification PIN to a stronger password option in 2026. The rollout and available settings may vary by account and app version, so follow the instructions displayed in your application.

Security tip: Use a unique password that you do not use on other websites. Keep your recovery email secure too.

3. Use Passkeys When Available

A passkey is a modern sign-in method that can use your device's fingerprint, face recognition or screen lock. It reduces the need to enter traditional passwords or verification codes during supported sign-in processes.

WhatsApp supports passkeys on compatible devices. To check whether the option is available, open WhatsApp Settings and look under Account and Passkeys. Follow the setup instructions shown on your device.

Never approve a sign-in request that you did not initiate. A passkey should be protected by your device's own screen lock.

4. Check Your Linked Devices Regularly

WhatsApp allows you to connect computers and other devices through its Linked Devices feature. This is useful, but an unfamiliar linked device can be a warning sign that someone has gained access.

How to review linked devices

  1. Open WhatsApp.
  2. Open Settings or the menu on your phone.
  3. Select Linked Devices.
  4. Review the devices and their recent activity.
  5. Tap any device you do not recognize and choose Log Out.

Never scan an unknown QR code. Scammers may pretend that you need to scan a code to verify, unlock or secure your account. This can link an attacker's device to your account.

5. Never Share Verification Codes

WhatsApp may send a registration code when someone tries to register your phone number. This code is private and should never be shared with another person.

Scammers may pretend to be a friend, customer support agent, delivery service or even a family member. They might claim that they accidentally sent a code to your phone and ask you to forward it.

  • Never share your WhatsApp SMS registration code.
  • Never share your two-step verification password or PIN.
  • Do not share email reset links or account recovery details.
  • Do not approve login requests you did not start.

Remember that a genuine friend should not need your private verification code to contact you.

6. Recognize Fake Links and Phishing Scams

Phishing is a technique in which criminals create fake websites or messages to trick people into revealing private information. WhatsApp users may receive messages containing suspicious links that promise prizes, free products, account verification or urgent security fixes.

Common warning signs

  • A message says your account will be permanently blocked unless you act immediately.
  • A website asks for your WhatsApp verification code.
  • A stranger offers free gifts or rewards in exchange for personal details.
  • A message asks you to scan a QR code to unlock your account.
  • A familiar contact suddenly requests money or sensitive information.
  • A website address contains spelling mistakes or an unfamiliar domain.

Before opening a suspicious link, verify the sender through another trusted communication method. Do not enter passwords, codes or payment details on websites reached through unexpected messages.

Remember: A familiar profile photo or contact name does not prove that a message is genuine. Someone may be impersonating a person you know.

7. Improve Your WhatsApp Privacy Settings

Privacy settings can help control who sees your personal information and who can contact or add you to groups.

Review these settings inside WhatsApp:

  • Last Seen and Online: Choose who can see when you were last active.
  • Profile Photo: Limit visibility to your contacts or another suitable audience.
  • About: Avoid sharing unnecessary personal information publicly.
  • Status: Share updates only with people you trust.
  • Groups: Restrict who can add you to groups.
  • Silence Unknown Callers: Consider using this feature to reduce unwanted calls.
  • App Lock: Use fingerprint, Face ID or another supported device security method.

You can find most of these options under Settings, then Privacy. Menu names and availability may differ slightly between Android and iPhone.

8. Keep WhatsApp and Your Phone Updated

Software updates can include important security fixes, bug corrections and new privacy protections. Using an outdated application or operating system may expose your device to avoidable security risks.

  • Download WhatsApp from Google Play or Apple's App Store.
  • Install official WhatsApp updates when available.
  • Keep Android or iOS updated.
  • Use a secure screen lock on your phone.
  • Avoid modified applications and unofficial WhatsApp versions.
  • Do not install unknown APK files from untrusted websites.

9. Protect Your Chat Backups

Chat backups may be stored in your Google Account or iCloud, depending on your device and backup settings. Protecting those accounts is also an important part of WhatsApp security.

WhatsApp offers end-to-end encrypted backup options. If you enable encrypted backups, carefully protect the password or encryption key required to restore them.

  • Use a strong password for your Google or Apple account.
  • Enable additional security on your cloud account.
  • Review which devices have access to your cloud account.
  • Consider enabling end-to-end encrypted backups.
  • Store backup recovery information somewhere safe.

Important: If you lose the password or encryption key for an encrypted backup, you may not be able to restore that backup. Keep recovery details secure.

10. What to Do If Your WhatsApp Account Is Hacked

If you suspect that someone has taken control of your WhatsApp account, act quickly. Do not panic or pay anyone who claims they can hack your account back.

Follow these recovery steps

  1. Open the official WhatsApp application and try to register your phone number again.
  2. Enter your full phone number and follow the instructions to receive the six-digit registration code by SMS or phone call.
  3. Enter the code in WhatsApp. Re-registering with the code automatically logs out other devices using your account.
  4. If asked for a two-step verification password or PIN that you do not know, follow WhatsApp's official recovery process. A waiting period may apply.
  5. Once you regain access, review Linked Devices and remove any unfamiliar sessions.
  6. Inform your friends and family that your account may have been compromised, especially if suspicious messages were sent.

If you no longer control your SIM card or phone number, contact your mobile network provider through its official support channel. If the recovery process fails, use the official WhatsApp Help Center.

Important recovery advice: Never give a recovery service your verification code, password or remote access to your phone. Use official WhatsApp recovery instructions only.

WhatsApp Security Checklist

Use this quick checklist to review your account security:

  • Two-step verification is enabled.
  • A secure recovery email is added where available.
  • Passkey is configured if supported and desired.
  • Linked Devices contains only recognized devices.
  • Verification codes are never shared.
  • Suspicious links and QR codes are avoided.
  • Privacy settings have been reviewed.
  • WhatsApp and the phone operating system are updated.
  • Cloud backups and recovery information are protected.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How can I protect my WhatsApp account from hackers?

Enable two-step verification, use a passkey where available, never share verification codes, review linked devices and keep your phone and WhatsApp updated.

2. Can someone hack my WhatsApp without my verification code?

Account access can be compromised through different methods, including malicious device linking, phishing or malware. Protect your phone, avoid suspicious QR codes and review linked devices regularly.

3. What should I do if I receive an unexpected WhatsApp code?

Do not share the code with anyone. An unexpected code may mean that someone has entered your phone number while attempting to register an account. Secure your account and ignore requests to forward the code.

4. How do I check if someone is using my WhatsApp account?

Open Settings and select Linked Devices. Review the list and log out of devices you do not recognize.

5. Is WhatsApp two-step verification free?

Yes. WhatsApp provides its account security features without requiring a separate payment. Always use the official app.

6. Can I recover my WhatsApp account after it is hacked?

In many cases, you can recover access by registering your phone number again with the official six-digit code. Follow WhatsApp's recovery instructions if an additional verification password is requested.

7. Should I scan a QR code sent by an unknown person?

No. Never scan an unfamiliar QR code to verify, unlock or secure your account. It may be used to link an unauthorized device to your WhatsApp account.

Final Thoughts

Protecting your WhatsApp account does not have to be complicated. A few basic security habits can make a meaningful difference. Enable two-step verification, protect your verification codes, check linked devices and avoid suspicious links or QR codes.

Most importantly, stay alert when someone creates urgency or asks for private information. Take a moment to verify unexpected requests before responding.
